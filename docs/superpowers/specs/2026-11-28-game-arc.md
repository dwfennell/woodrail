# The game's arc: three wonders, built toward from the first hour

Status: agreed with the user 2026-11-28 (direction A, all three wonders, fixed order, every map, a far shore of a
different kind). Nothing built yet. The build steps are in section 9.

## 1. Why

The user, after 2-10.5 h of play: "the game kind of feels like it's not building to anything... once you figure out a
method for making chains of two or three things that is pretty much the same challenge. I wish there was a more well
defined end goal, or at least more gameplay variety somehow."

What their saves show (seed 770187, 2.2 h to 10.5 h; the rowan/dandelion game to 11 h; a read-only summary of every
Game save in the repo: towns' levels, industries by kind, trains, grand plans):

- **After the first City only the amounts change.** Towns grow on six goods (`TOWN_LEVELS`, `townLevel`): planks,
  flour, stone, then iron, furniture and tools, each level after City wanting 1.5× the last. Nothing new is asked
  from about 4 h on.
- **The deepest chain is three steps** (logs → planks → furniture; coal + ore → iron → tools).
- **A third of the industries were never built.** At 10.5 h: 23 kinds of 33. Never: orchard, juicer, creamery, ice
  cream, sugar beet, jam, chocolate, glass, toys, cars, oil, water. Nothing needs them; they only sell, at half price
  (`STAPLES`, 2026-11-27).
- **The map stops changing.** The same 9 towns and 12 plots from 8.5 h. Five of the 9 towns were hamlets until 10 h.
  The other game spread to 12 towns and 16 plots, with 6 sawmills, and its best town was City ★.
- **Grand projects are the one long goal, and the user took them** (the Exhibition done, the Cathedral 3 of 4 stages,
  the Royal Train started). Staged builds fed by trains are the thing to build on.
- **Coins 7 k → 43 k** with nothing to spend them on that changes play (the coin-sinks spec,
  `2026-11-28-late-game-coin-sinks.md`, which this spec works with: section 8).

The October direction doc's "Building up to the rocket" (`2026-10-15-game-direction-design.md` §4) was the old plan
for an end. The user chose a different set: three wonders that fit a wooden toy railway, each with a small celebration
and something that keeps paying after.

## 2. The shape

Three **wonders** on every Game map, visible from the first minute, opened and built one after another:

| Act | Wonder | Opens | Built by about | Its day | After |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Summit Railway**: a hotel on the map's peak | the first City | 5-7 h | the first train up at dusk, the hotel's windows lit | a tourist line, the hotel buying treats |
| 2 | **The Flyer**: a record-breaking express | the Summit open and a City ★★ | 8-9 h | the record run on your longest line, its time on a board | the Flyer is yours, the best train in the game |
| 3 | **The Bridge to the Far Shore** | the Flyer built | 10-12 h | the Flyer's first crossing: bunting, boats' horns, fireworks | the far shore: new land, new towns that want new things |

Under them:

- **Specialties per star** (direction C's slice): from City ★ each star asks for one specialty of its own, so the
  unused chains are needed and running by the time the wonders need their goods.
- **Grand projects per city** (the coin-sinks spec's A) are steps toward Act 3.
- **The Journal's Wonders page** shows all three from the start: what each needs to open, what it takes, what it
  gives. The player always knows what's next.

The game never ends. Each wonder's day is a short scene (section 5), a sticker and a keepsake, and then play goes on,
with more to do than before (the hotel's treats, the Flyer's fares, the far shore).

### Hour by hour (a player like the user)

- **0-1 h.** The starter line. The peak stands on the map's edge with its hotel in mist (as a secret site's mist).
  Tapped: a card, "The Summit Railway: a hotel on the peak. Opens when a town is a City." The Journal's Wonders page
  shows three silhouettes.
- **2-4 h.** Farms, mills, quarries, the first City. **The Summit opens**: its card now says what the hotel's first
  stage takes, and that a line has to reach its halt. The plot it stands on is for sale like any other.
- **4-7 h.** The climb: the rack, tunnels, a viaduct (tech tree 5, which the user had mostly learned by 9 h but used
  little). The hotel goes up in four stages. **Its day.** Then tourists ride up at a high fare, and the hotel asks
  for treats each month: ice cream, jam, chocolate, juice (the first real reason for those chains).
- **6-9 h.** City ★ and ★★, each asking for its specialty. **The Flyer opens** beside a City ★★: a works where it is
  built in stages. Its boiler needs **steel** (new: a steelworks, iron + coal). **Its day**: the record run.
- **8-12 h.** Grand projects per city. **The Bridge opens** when the Flyer is built: its site at the coast (or a
  gorge), its stages taking stone, bricks, steel, glass, paint, and two cities with a grand project done. **Its day**
  is the Flyer's first crossing.
- **After.** The far shore: plots across the bridge, with towns, an orchard coast and a cocoa port, whose towns grow on
  fruit, fish and chocolate as well as the six. A second, smaller arc there: more grand projects, more fairs, more
  overhauls (the coin-sinks spec), the Flyer's fares.

## 3. The wonders

Coin prices are pitched at the income the user had at each point (about 2-3 k an hour at 5 h, 5-9 k at 8 h, 10 k+ at
10 h): each stage between 10 and 40 minutes of income, so a wonder is a real thing to save for (coin-sinks spec §3,
"something to save up for late"). Goods are pitched against grand projects' (12-30 a good a stage). The numbers are
first guesses, settled by the balance sweep and the late band (section 7).

### 3.1 Act 1: the Summit Railway

**The peak.** Every new Game map has one: a tall hill (the highest on the map, 5-6 levels) on a plot for sale at the
edge of the land, two plots from the start's 2×2, with the hotel's site on top and a halt beside it. On Flat and
Gentle maps it's the one big hill there is. On an old save the peak is placed on the nearest plot not yet bought
(plots are generated from the seed when bought: `plotLand`), else the highest hill on the land the player owns
(it's never made by raising land under track).

**Reaching it** is the challenge: a line up the hill by ramps, racks, tunnels, a viaduct, or the lift. All exist.
The halt is laid as fixed kit track when the site is reached (as a secret site's: `secretsReached`), and it's a
sight stop (`isSightStop`): riders from every town go there.

**Stages**, goods brought by train to the halt:

| Stage | Name | Goods | Coins |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 0 | The site cleared | (opens; nothing) | 0 |
| 1 | The foundations | stone 24, planks 20 | 500 |
| 2 | The lodge | bricks 16, planks 16, iron 8 | 1 000 |
| 3 | The glass dining room | glass 12, furniture 10 | 1 500 |
| 4 | Opening night | bread 10, icecream 8, 80 riders to the summit | 0 |

**Its day.** At dusk: the camera on the hotel as the first train up arrives, the windows light, bunting on the halt.
About 8 seconds; a tap skips it.

**After.** Tourists pay 3× the plain fare to the summit (the lookout halt's rule, `isSightStop`). The hotel has a
monthly order for treats (one of ice cream, jam, chocolate, juice, cheese a month, 8-12 of it); filled, the month's
tourists pay 4× and a coin bonus. Never filled costs nothing. A sticker: Summit Railway.

### 3.2 Act 2: the Flyer

**Opens** when the Summit is built and a town is City ★★. The **works** (a yard with a long shed, its own model) is
placed by a City ★★, like a grand project's plot.

**Steel**: a steelworks (new industry, Tech tree tier 3, needs Foundry): iron 1 + coal 1 → steel 1. It's the Flyer's
and the Bridge's good, and a City ★★★'s specialty (section 4).

| Stage | Name | Goods | Coins |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | The frames | steel 16, iron 12 | 3 000 |
| 2 | The boiler | steel 20, coal 20, tools 10 | 4 000 |
| 3 | The streamlined casing | paint 14, glass 10 | 4 000 |
| 4 | The carriages | furniture 14, planks 20, bread 10 | 3 000 |

**Its day: the record run.** The Flyer is set going on the player's longest line (the line with the most track on its
lap; a line is needed, said on the card). The camera follows it once round, the stations it passes have a crowd, and
the time goes on a board at the works. Nothing else is held: it runs as a train runs, signals and all, so a jammed
line makes a slow time (the run's time is the lap's, measured, shown, never a fail).

**After.** The Flyer (an engine and three carriages in its own livery, in the toy box: as the Royal Train's gift,
`ROYAL_GIFT`): the fastest engine (1.4× an express's top speed), pulls 6, and its riders pay 1.5×. One only, never
made at the workshop. Its record can be run again from the works' card on any line: the best time is kept.

### 3.3 Act 3: the Bridge to the Far Shore

**Opens** when the Flyer is built. Its site is at the coast (a map with a sea), across from the far shore that's
been in view all game. A map with No sea gets a **gorge** at its edge instead: a great river valley with the far
land beyond. The site is fixed per map, from the seed.

| Stage | Name | Goods | Other | Coins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1 | The piers | stone 40, bricks 20 | | 8 000 |
| 2 | The steel spans | steel 40, iron 20 | | 12 000 |
| 3 | The deck and rails | planks 30, tools 16 | two cities with a grand project done | 12 000 |
| 4 | The lamps and paint | glass 16, paint 16 | | 8 000 |

**Its day.** The Flyer makes the first crossing: bunting along the bridge, the harbour's boats sound their horns,
fireworks over the water at dusk. About 10 seconds; a tap skips it. The sticker: Bridge to the Far Shore.

**After: the far shore.** The bridge's far end is track the player lays on. The far shore is a fixed region of
plots, bought as plots are (`plotPrice`), with land of a different kind: warm colours, orchards and palms by the
shore, a cocoa port, fishing villages. Its towns grow on fruit, fish and chocolate (with planks, stone and the rest);
cocoa comes from its port (today only from abroad). How big, and how it's drawn, is step 6's own design note: it has
to stay inside the phone's memory budget (section 7).

## 4. Specialties per star

Today a City ★, ★★, ★★★ wants the same goods ×1.5 a level (`townLevel`). With this, each star past City also asks for
**one specialty**, picked for that town when it reaches the star (from its seed; never two neighbouring towns the
same, so lines between them matter):

| Star | One of |
| --- | --- |
| ★ | cheese, juice, jam, bread |
| ★★ | toys, chocolate, ice cream, bricks |
| ★★★ | glass, steel, cars |
| ★★★★ and on | two of the above, one from each of the last two rows |

The amount is the star's furniture want (6, 9, 14…). The town card's goods bars show it, Next up names its maker if
the map has none (`town-next.ts`'s `maker` and `research` kinds already do this). Saved: the picks, per town (a save
version bump and fixtures).

It changes the late game's goods, so the balance sweep's bands, the late band and the play-through must be checked.
The play-through needs a path that builds the chains: see section 7.

## 4b. Depth in the tech tree (the user: "our tech tree has breadth but not really that much depth")

Measured from `BLUEPRINTS` (127 rows):

- **Prerequisites run deep, but most rows are leaves.** The longest chain of needs is 7 rows, yet **70 of the 127 rows
  are needed by nothing**. A row is learned, gives one thing, and nothing later builds on it.
- **Goods chains stop at three steps**, and every product is an end: furniture, tools, toys, jam and the rest go
  into a town and nowhere else. Nothing made is the input of something made later, except planks and iron.
- **A row rarely changes how you play.** Most give an industry, a wagon or a place. The ones the user's play changed
  around (switches, signals, routes, depot tracks, warehouses) are few.

To add depth (to settle with the user, then folded into the steps below):

1. **Intermediate goods** so chains reach 4-5 steps, used by the wonders' late stages and the top stars: steel (iron +
   coal); parts (steel + tools); and later products that take a made good as input (cars from steel + parts + glass;
   clockwork toys from toys + parts). Each product gets a second use as well as a town's: a wonder stage, a specialty,
   an upgrade.
2. **Rows that build on rows**: a learned industry gets a second-level row that changes it (a sawmill that also makes
   furniture from its own planks; a foundry that makes steel; a farm co-op that feeds a dairy and a mill from one
   yard), so earlier choices keep paying and combine.
3. **Wonders as stems**: each wonder needs a set of rows across branches (the Summit: rack, tunnels, lookout halt; the
   Flyer: steelworks, express engines, signals; the Bridge: viaduct, steel, a harbour), so leaves become roads to
   something.

## 5. The wonder framework (shared)

One module, `src/game/wonders.ts`, beside `grand.ts`, and its UI:

- **Data**: `WONDERS` (id, name, opens-when, site rule, stages of goods, coins, riders, other needs, day, keepsake,
  after-reward), as `GRANDS` is.
- **State**: `Economy.wonders: id → { stage, got, opened, day?, best? }`, saved with the world. A save version bump; an
  old-save fixture (no wonders: they're placed on load as section 3.1 says) and one at the maximum (all built, the
  Flyer's best time).
- **The site**: a prop per stage (`wonder-<id>-<stage>`), in mist till opened (as hidden secret sites). Its halt (the
  Summit) or yard (the works) is a depot of role `wonder`, a sink for its stage's goods (as a school's research is:
  goods are the progress; unpaid, as grand stages are: `grandLacks`). The Bridge's goods go to a yard at its landward
  end.
- **The card**: what opens it, the stage under way with its bars, Start (coins paid), what it gives. 44 px buttons,
  0 frames while open.
- **The Journal's Wonders page**: the three, in order, each a picture, its state, Show me (the camera to its site).
- **Its day**: a scene played by `camera-director.ts` (a few framed shots and one follow), the props it puts up
  (bunting, lights, fireworks as a few instanced sprites) taken down after. Skippable by a tap, played once, never
  replayed on load. Draws only while it plays.
- **Goals**: one Journal goal per act ("Open the Summit Railway", "Build the Flyer", "Cross to the far shore"), each
  reached in the play-through by the player's path.
- **Stickers**: one per wonder's day; the Flyer's record tiers.

Grand projects keep their own code; the coin-sinks session is making them per city (keyed so "done anywhere on the
map" and "done in city X" are both cheap to read). The Bridge's "two cities with a grand project done" reads that.

## 6. Every map, adapted

- **Hills.** The peak is placed on every map (Flat included). On Mountainous maps it's simply the tallest hill.
- **Water.** With a sea, the Bridge spans it to the far shore. With No sea, it spans a gorge at the map's edge.
- **Sandbox** has no wonders (it has no goals or coins). Its tray may offer the wonders' finished models as
  decorations later.
- **Old saves**: wonders are placed on load (the peak on a plot not yet bought, else the owned land's highest hill;
  the Bridge's site from the seed). A game past City is told once: "Three wonders are on your map now", with Show me.

## 7. Phones, balance, testing

- **Draw cost.** A wonder is one static model a stage (a city's biggest building in size and triangles, measured with
  `node tools/shots/yard-buildings.mjs`), `gpuOnly`. Mist as the secret sites draw it. A day's scene draws only while
  it plays; nothing per frame after. 0 idle frames. `npm run perf:check` and `perf:memory` on the features map with
  each wonder built; the far shore's terrain and towns are budgeted in step 6.
- **Balance.** The play-through (`src/play/balance.test.ts`) reaches the Summit's opening and its first stage by the
  player's path within its 300-minute run. The later acts need a longer play-through that builds the chains
  (specialties, steel): a `BALANCE_MINUTES=720` run with a chain-building player, added in step 3, with bands for
  when each act opens. Each act's goal is reached by the player's path, or allowlisted with a reason until that run
  exists.
- **Routing.** The Flyer is a faster engine: the routing cases, `npm run jams` and the fuzz in step 4.
- **E2E**, on a 390×844 phone by taps, each asserting the payoff: the Summit opened, a line laid up to its halt, a
  stage's goods delivered and the stage built, its day played, tourists paying 3×; the works and a stage of the Flyer,
  the record run timed; the Bridge's stage built, the crossing, track laid on the far shore; a town at ★ asking for its
  specialty, a train bringing it, the town growing.
- **Saves.** Each saved addition has a fixture at its maximum and an old-save fixture (`src/persist/corpus.test.ts`,
  `save-maxima.test.ts`, `save-limits.test.ts`'s `REACH` rows).

## 8. With the coin-sinks plan

The coin-sinks session (`2026-11-28-late-game-coin-sinks.md`) builds, in order: early help, table changes (tier-3
research coins ×2, dearer land past 12 plots), grand projects per city, town fairs, engine overhauls. They fit under
the arc:

- Grand projects per city feed Act 3 ("two cities with a grand project done").
- The wonders' coin prices (3-12 k a stage) are late sinks of the kind the coin-sinks spec wants: priced goals 30-90
  minutes of income away that change the railway. Its measures (coins in hand under about 1.5 h of income) are judged
  with both in.
- Overhauls and the Flyer: the Flyer is never overhauled (it's already the best).
- Fairs and the hotel's treats both double fares for a while: they don't stack (the higher applies).

## 9. Build steps

Each step lands on its own, playable and green (`/check` by its tier), on its own branch.

1. **The framework and the Summit's site** (`claude/arc-1-wonders`): `WONDERS` with the Summit only, its state and
   saves, the peak on new maps and placed on old saves, the mist, the card (opens at the first City: Start, stages,
   bars), the halt found by track, stage goods by train, stage props (placeholder models from existing ones: a lodge
   from the row models, the observatory's), the Journal's Wonders page, the goal. E2E `wonders`. Fixtures.
2. **The Summit's day and after**: the hotel's models (Blender), its day's scene, tourists at 3×, the monthly treats
   order, the sticker. Perf and memory checks.
3. **Specialties per star**, and the long play-through (720 min, a chain-building player) with bands for each act.
   Balance sweep, late band.
4. **Steel and the Flyer**: the steelworks (research row, recipe, model, wiki page and picture), the works, its
   stages, the record run, the Flyer in the toy box. Routing cases, jams, fuzz.
5. **The Bridge**: its site (coast or gorge), stages, the crossing's day. The far end as a closed stub till step 6.
6. **The far shore**: its own short design note first (land, towns, what they want, cocoa, the budget), then the build.
