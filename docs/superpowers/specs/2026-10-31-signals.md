# Signals you place on the track (2026-10-31, issue #16)

## Status

A design, not built. No code yet. Rail and road team.

**The user's words:** "Semaphores and signals should be easier to set up, maybe do something more similar to OpenTTD."
And on the issue: "place signals onto existing track to control access to that part of the track. These are block
signals the player places."

**The bar from the routing spec (§16):** no turn-backs except at the end of the line; a wait that can't end is a stuck
card, not a turn; lines pass at loops; points are locked for the train that set them. Signals must not undo any of it.

## 1. What exists today

- **Signals are track pieces.** `signal` (a 144 mm straight, `PieceDef.signal = { stopAt: 100 }`) on the Switch
  shelf, and three kit skins of it (`kit-semaphore-signal`, `kit-colour-light-signal`, `kit-signal-gantry`, in
  `src/track/kits.ts`). To add one, the player must lay it into the line: take up a straight, close the gap. Kit signals
  don't splice (`KitDef.splices` is off). This is the main reason they're hard to set up.
- **One way only.** A signal guards trains running from connector 0 to 1. Which way it faces is the way the piece was
  laid (the tray's flip).
- **The sim does all of it** (`src/sim/sim.ts`, "signals: block working"). Each tick, `updateSignals` walks each
  signal's block: from its far end to the next signal, **switch or crossing**, or the end. Red while a train is on it or
  while held by hand (a bit in the piece's switch state, `toggleSignal`). `signalCap` brakes a train to the stop point
  of a red signal within `SIGNAL_LOOK` (300 mm). `signalsVersion` makes the view redraw only on a change.
- **Taps:** a tap on a signal holds it at red or lets it go (the plain one's post, a skin's model via `signalRoadAt`,
  or a chip on a signal box's card). The card says "Waiting at a red signal" (`engine-status.ts`).
- **Sections, the router and stalls don't know signals.** Sections never grant a move into a line-mate, so a line's
  train doesn't meet a red set by a line-mate (crossing-rethink §"Signals"). The router ignores signal pieces
  (`isPoints`). Stalls treat a red signal as a wait that ends on its own (`stalls.ts`), which is wrong for one held by
  hand.
- **Stale words.** Since step 5c lines pass at loops with no signals, but the Signals blueprint still says "Two
  trains share a line", the second engine says "Needs Signals to share a line" (`crafting.ts` `ENGINE_NOTE`), and the
  "two-trains" goal still `needs: ["signals"]`.
- **Not a block signal:** the toy semaphore `TrackView` draws beside each switch shows which way the points lie. It
  stays as it is.

## 2. Who signals are for

- **A young kid never needs one.** Everything in routing §8 works with none: lines, loops, routes, points set and put
  back, trains waiting at points and at loops. Nothing tells a player to place a signal unless one would clearly help
  (§7).
- **A player who wants control** places one with one drag from the tray (or one tap with the Signal tool held), on any
  track already laid, and sees at once which stretch it guards.
- **What a signal is for, in the game:** (1) letting trains of a line follow each other through a long stretch instead
  of waiting for the whole of it to clear; (2) keeping trains apart on an oval or a run with no line; (3) choosing where
  a train waits before a junction (off a level crossing, clear of a station); (4) the toy: tap it red, the train stops.
- **Signals refine the automatic system, never fight it.** With no signals placed, everything runs exactly as today
  (trajectory hashes identical). A signal only adds a boundary or a waiting point; it never lets a train go where
  sections or the router would hold it.

## 3. The model: a signal is a mark at a joint

A signal stops being a piece. It is a mark on a joint between two pieces already laid, facing one way.

```ts
interface Signal {
  id: number;
  at: number;          // a connectorRef: the joint, on the side trains come from (the approach piece)
  style: "plain" | "semaphore" | "colour-light";
  held: boolean;       // held at red by hand
}
// Layout.signals: Map<id, Signal>; Layout.signalsVersion bumps on add, remove, flip, style
```

- **At joints only.** Wooden pieces are short, so joints are close enough, and a joint is exact: no position along a
  curve to store or snap. Never inside points or a crossing (they have no inner joints). A joint can take one signal
  each way.
- **The stop point** is `STAND_SHORT` before the joint, on the approach piece. The post stands beside the joint on the
  driver's right (as the plain signal's does).
- **Facing:** it guards trains crossing the joint from the approach piece. Trains the other way pass it and ignore it.
- **Removed with its joint:** a layout edit that takes up either piece or breaks the joint drops the mark (undo puts it
  back with the piece).

### 3.1 Blocks

- **A block** is the track you can reach from a signal's far side, every way through points, until you meet any
  signal (either facing), a buffer or an open end. As in OpenTTD, a block can contain points.
- **A plain block** (no points in it): red while any train is on it, or while held. This is today's block signal.
- **A junction block** (points in it) is a **path signal**: red unless the train coming to it has its way through
  locked by the router up to its next clear point (routing §3.2), and that way is free of trains. Two trains can be in
  one junction block on roads that don't meet. The router already does this at every junction for routed and hand-run
  trains (steps 4 and 7); the signal moves where they wait, back to the signal, and shows it.
- **One type, not two.** The player never picks "block" or "path". A signal is smart: plain track gives a block
  signal, points give a path signal. (Open question 2.)
- Blocks are a per-layout derived map, worked out again only when the track or `Layout.signals` changes
  (`signalsVersion`), cached like `hillsOf(grid)`.

### 3.2 How signals map onto sections and slots

Sections (`src/economy/sections.ts`) stay the authority on lines. A signal adds to them:

- **A waiting point is a one-way slot.** A signal whose approach has room for the network's longest train plus
  `ROAD_MARGIN`, clear of points behind it, makes a slot for trains going its way: `Slot.kind = "signal"`, `dir` set. It
  splits the section it's in into two sections. For trains going against it, the two sections and the slot stay one
  section (they need all of it, so two trains never meet head on).
- **Moves are unchanged:** a train asks for the section ahead and a slot at its far end. A follower going the same way
  can now be granted the first half as soon as the leader is past the signal, instead of waiting for the leader to
  reach the far slot.
- **The room rule is unchanged.** Signals add no room: a one-way slot can't let trains pass, and a convoy needs room at
  the far end anyway. So a signal never lets the room rule admit a train that could then be stuck.
- **A signal short of room** (too short for the longest train) is a block boundary only: the sim holds trains at it,
  sections don't use it as a slot. Its card in long-press says so ("Too short for a train to wait here").
- **Aspects follow the grant.** For a line's train, a signal ahead of it shows clear only once sections granted the
  move beyond it (and, for a junction block, the router locked the way). So the green the player sees is the truth.

### 3.3 Router locks

- A signal before points is the router's wait point for trains coming that way: the train stands at the signal's stop
  point, not at `STAND_SHORT + OVERLAP` short of the fouling end. Within `SET_LOOK` of the signal it asks for its locks
  (all or none, to the next clear point, which a waiting-point signal also is).
- The router never sets or holds a signal. A signal held by hand is a wait the router respects (routing §15): a train
  stands at it with its locks not yet taken, so it holds nothing ahead.
- Hand-run trains (step 7) are unchanged except where they stand: at the signal.

### 3.4 Trains no line manages

A lone train, a hand-run train, a train on no line, an oval with no switches: the sim's block working as today, with
the new blocks. A lone train on an unheld layout never meets red.

## 4. Placing them on a phone

- **Where:** the Signal is on the Switch shelf (after Signals research in Game mode; always in Sandbox). Its styles
  (plain, semaphore, colour light) are one item with a style chip on its long-press card, not three tray items.
- **Drag from the tray** onto the track: the ghost snaps to the nearest joint within `SNAP_CAPTURE_RADIUS` of the
  finger, on the track the finger is over. Off track, no snap and the usual "Drop it onto the track" toast.
- **Which way it faces:** the way the finger last moved along the track, so dragging along the line in the direction
  of travel faces it right. A round arrow chip beside the ghost flips it. After placing, it can be turned from its
  card.
- **One tap per signal more:** with the Signal tool picked from the tray (a tap on it, not a drag), each tap on a joint
  places one, facing away from the camera's side of the track; a drag along the track places one at each joint passed,
  every Nth joint (N chosen so each gap is at least the longest train). Tap the tool again to put it down.
- **Taps on a placed signal:** hold at red / let go, as today (the toy, kept for kids). Turning round and style are on
  the card. (Open question 1: OpenTTD flips on a tap.)
- **Long-press** opens its card: "Signal: guards this way →", Turn round, Hold at red, Style, Remove, and a line if
  it's too short to wait at.
- **Blocks are visible while it matters:** while a signal is being dragged, or its card is open, its block ahead is
  tinted green over the track, the block behind it (the one it ends) blue. One overlay mesh, built from the blocks'
  pieces when the snap target changes (cached by joint and facing), removed on drop or close. No overlay otherwise.
- **Moving one:** a drag that starts on a placed signal picks it up (as pieces are picked up) and drops it on another
  joint.
- **Undo** is the layout's undo (one step per signal, or one per drag-placed run).

## 5. How they look

- **Plain:** today's toy semaphore post (from `TrackView.signalPost`). **Semaphore** and **colour light**: the kit
  models, without their slab of track (a mark has no track of its own).
- **Aspects:** the semaphore arm glides between level (red) and raised (clear) in about 0.3 s; the colour light swaps
  an emissive material (red or green). No light objects, no bloom.
- **Cheap to draw:** posts are one `InstancedMesh` per style; arms one per style, their matrices written only on an
  aspect change; colour-light heads two instanced draws (red and green), each instance moved between them on a
  change. A few draws however many signals.
- **0 idle frames:** aspects change only on events (§8), bump `signalsVersion`, and the game draws only then and
  during the glide. Arms don't cast shadows (posts do, and never move), so a change never calls `moveShadows()`.

## 6. Trains at signals: cards

- "Waiting at a red signal" stays for a short wait.
- **Held by hand:** after `STUCK_S`, "Waiting at a signal you're holding. Tap it to let go". Not counted as a stall.
- **Waiting at a signal for a train ahead:** the chain goes on to that train (`Sim.heldBy` gains `"signal"`, naming
  the block's occupant, or the router's holder for a junction block). So stalls see rings through signals.
- **A ring of signals** (an oval with as many trains as blocks: each stands at a red with a train in the next block):
  "Stuck: too many trains for these signals. Add a signal or put one away". Only placed signals can cause this; with
  none, followers on an oval just follow (`followRoom`).

## 7. Hints that mention signals

Only where a signal would help, and only after the player has learned Signals:

- **A line's trains following each other** stand waiting for a line-mate going the same way for more than 20 s, on a
  section long enough to split (room for the longest train on each side): once ever, "Trains on this line wait for the
  whole stretch. A signal here lets them follow", with "Show me where" pointing at the middle joint.
- Nowhere else. The head-on tip, the loop cards and §8 keep saying nothing about signals.
- **Fix the stale words** (with Game Mode): the Signals blurb ("Place signals on any track to split it into blocks:
  trains follow each other closer. Tap one to hold trains there."), `ENGINE_NOTE` removed, the "two-trains" goal's
  `needs: ["signals"]` becomes `["switches"]` (open question 5).

## 8. Working out aspects, and cost

- **On events, not every tick.** Today `updateSignals` walks every block every tick. New: each block keeps the set of
  trains on it, updated when a train's front or tail crosses a piece boundary (the sim already notices that for
  `piecesUnder`). An aspect is worked out again only when its block's occupancy, a hold, a grant or a lock on its way
  changes. A tick with no such change costs one version check.
- **Placement:** a pointer move finds the nearest joint from the snap grid the tray already uses, then looks up the
  cached block for the tint. Target: under 1 ms on a phone (measured in the e2e, `performance.now()` around it).
- **Blocks and slots:** worked out on a track edit or signal change, with sections' topology (which is already redone
  then). A signal change redoes the topology but no geometry, no colliders, no track meshes.
- **Draw:** a few instanced draws total (§5). 0 idle frames.
- **Memory:** a signal is four numbers; a block is a piece list.
- **No signals:** every path returns at once (`Layout.signals.size === 0` and no legacy pieces).

## 9. Old signal pieces and saves

- **Saves:** `signals?: [at, style, held][]`, at as piece id and connector index (never car ids). Old saves have none.
- **Old signal pieces stay pieces.** They load and run unchanged as track; the sim reads each one's `def.signal` as a
  mark at its far joint, with its stop point where it is today (`stopAt` 100), held from its switch-state bits. The
  gantry gives one mark per road. One block-working path for both.
- **Their blocks change** (they now run on through points, as path signals). Measured on sections QA and the
  play-throughs at step 2; expected drift only where a signal piece stands short of points.
- **The tray** stops offering the signal pieces and the three skins once the Signal mark lands (step 4). Pieces in a
  save can still be picked up and moved, and their models still show aspects. (Open question 3: convert them to a plain
  straight plus a mark on load instead.)
- **Cloud saves** merge `signals` like other layout fields (Sandbox owns `cloud.ts`; a one-line addition).

## 10. Tests

### 10.1 Harness (`src/routing/harness.ts`)

- **Scenario builder:** `signal(pieceId, connector, { held? })` in `build`, and events `holdSignal(id, on)`,
  `turnSignal(id)`, `removeSignal(id)`.
- **New invariants, every tick:**
  - `redRun`: no train's front axle crosses a signal's joint while it's red for that train, unless it was already
    within braking distance when it turned red (and then it's counted, and must be 0 for a hold set by the router or
    sections).
  - `blockOne`: a plain block never has two trains in it that entered through its signal.
  - `noSignalsSame`: every existing case with no signals keeps its trajectory hash.
- **Cases:**
  - `ovalConvoy`: 3 trains, 4 signals on an oval: gaps kept, all deliver. With 3 signals: stuck, kind "signals", said
    after `STUCK_S`, nobody turns.
  - `lineFollowers`: one line, long single track between two loops, 2 trains the same way. Without the signal the
    follower waits for the whole section; with one in the middle it leaves when the leader passes it. Payoff:
    arrivals per 30 min go up.
  - `againstSignal`: a train going against a waiting-point signal takes the whole section and never stops at it; one
    coming the other way waits at the far slot (no head-on, no turn).
  - `pathAtJunction`: a signal 200 mm before points: a routed train stands at the signal while another holds the
    points; its aspect is red then, green once its locks are granted; `wrongRoad` 0.
  - `heldByHand`: held 40 s, train waits all of it, its card says "holding", no stall; let go, it goes.
  - `legacyPiece`: an old `signal` piece on plain track holds trains as today (same hash where no points are in its
    block).
  - `shortWaitingPoint`: a signal with too little room makes no slot; sections run as without it.
  - `saveMidBlock`, `pieceTakenUp` (its mark goes; trains run as without).
- **Fuzz:** `harness-fuzz.test.ts` gains a seed set that places 0 to 6 random marks on the loop fixtures: all
  invariants hold; a stall only with a signal ring.

### 10.2 Player-path e2e

`E2E_ONLY=signals` in `scripts/e2e/touch.mjs`, Game mode on a 390×844 phone. Setup may learn Signals and Switches
(not the thing under test). Then, all by the player's actions:

1. A line of two trains between two loops (drawn and routed by taps, as `stuck-lines` does).
2. The Signal dragged from the Switch shelf onto the middle of the long stretch: the ghost snaps to a joint, the block
   tint is drawn (an overlay mesh visible), placement cost under 1 ms a move (median).
3. Dropped. The tint goes. Long-press: its card, Turn round shows the arrow flip, turned back.
4. **Payoff:** the follower leaves its loop while the leader is still in the stretch, and both deliver. Deliveries in
   4 game minutes beat the same layout with no signal (measured in the same run, signal removed by its card).
5. Tap: held at red, the next train stops at it, card "Waiting at a red signal"; after 5 s, "… you're holding". Tap:
   it goes.
6. 0 idle frames with signals placed and the game paused.
7. An old save with a `kit-semaphore-signal` piece loads, and its signal still holds a train.

## 11. Steps (each lands alone)

1. **Marks in the data.** `Layout.signals`, save and load, undo, prune on track edits; old pieces read as marks. The
   sim's block working reads the one list, blocks as today (ending at points). No behaviour change: all hashes
   identical. Harness builder and `redRun`, `blockOne`.
2. **Blocks through points, aspects on events.** The new blocks (§3.1) for marks and old pieces; junction blocks wait
   on router locks; `Sim.heldBy` "signal". Harness `ovalConvoy`, `pathAtJunction`, `heldByHand`, `legacyPiece`.
   Measure sections QA and play-through drift, and tick cost before and after.
3. **Sections' waiting points.** One-way signal slots, aspects follow grants. `lineFollowers`, `againstSignal`,
   `shortWaitingPoint`. Sections QA identical with no signals.
4. **Placing on a phone.** Tray Signal, snap at joints, facing by drag, the flip chip, the Signal tool, long-press
   card, block tint. Signal pieces leave the tray. e2e parts 1 to 3.
5. **How they look.** Instanced posts, arms and heads, glide, 0 idle frames. e2e part 6.
6. **Cards and hints.** Held-by-hand card, signal ring stall, the follow hint, stale words (with Game Mode). e2e parts 4,
   5 and 7. `balance.test.ts`: no goal needs a signal; the goals player never places one.

## 13. Open questions for the user

1. **A tap on a placed signal:** hold it at red (as now, fun for kids), or turn it round (as in OpenTTD)? We suggest:
   tap holds, turn round from its card.
2. **One kind of signal** that acts as a block signal on plain track and a path signal before points, or two kinds to
   choose from, as in OpenTTD? We suggest one.
3. **Old signal pieces:** leave them as pieces that still work, or turn them into a plain straight with a signal on
   load? We suggest leave them.
4. **Signals facing both ways** at one joint as one signal, or place two? We suggest two (simpler to read).
5. **Signals research:** keep it in Game mode, with new words ("trains follow each other closer")? And the "Run two
   trains" goal no longer waits for it (loops are enough now)?
6. **A "show all blocks" button** on the map, or only while a signal is held or its card is open? We suggest only then.
7. **The gantry:** keep it as a piece, or draw one by itself where two signals stand side by side on double track?
