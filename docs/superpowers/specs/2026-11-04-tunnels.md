# Tunnels: track under the hills and under the felt

Phase 1 was design, a prototype and pictures. Phase 2 (built, branch `claude/tunnels`) is option A with the owner's change: hills **one level** high can be tunnelled too (see "Built (phase 2)" at the end, which supersedes the phase 1 rule where they differ).

The user: "tunnels being able to go underground a bit when it makes sense (similar to the new crossing track bridge) is useful... the question is what happens on the other side of the hill. We could also make it so you can tunnel down into flat ground."

Tunnels is Bridges' only child in the tech tree (planned, "soon"; `2026-10-31-tech-tree.md`).

## Measurements

All heights in mm from the track's base.

- **A level** is 48. Hills are 0 to 3 levels (144). Squares are 72.
- **The tallest car** stands 72.5 over its track (the double-decker; `TRAIN_HEADROOM` is 76). So a tunnel needs **two levels of ground over its track (96)**: one level (48) is 24.5 short. Two leave a 20 mm roof.
- **Hills step a level a square.** Map generation ("no more than one above a neighbour") and the Raise tool both keep every square within one level of its four neighbours. So a line on the felt never meets two levels of hill at once: it always meets **a one-level step first**, at least a square deep, before the hill is high enough to bore. Any tunnel into a hill starts and ends in a cutting. This is the answer to "what happens on the other side": the same thing as on this side.
- **Down into the felt**, a line on top at ground level needs 76 over the tunnel's track: the tunnel must go **two levels down (−96)**, not one. (One level down, −48, with a line on the felt over it is 28 short, the same shortfall as the low bridge.) A ramp's grade (steepest 0.5, eased) takes 288 to go down 96: two ramps' length.

## The rule (all options share it)

With Tunnels learned:

1. **Track under two levels of ground is in a tunnel** (`TUNNEL_COVER` = 96). Anything may be over it: a hill, the felt, a town, a road, a river, other track (96 between bases clears the trains). Its track and trains are hidden by the ground itself.
2. **A plain straight cuts through one level of hill** (a cutting): a slot with upright sides, 62 wide, through the one-level blocks only. The two-level hill beyond stays whole.
3. **A portal** stands wherever the line goes from open air into two levels of ground: at the end of the cutting, on the two-level block's side. Hills are square blocks, so the line must meet the side **square** (along x or z); at a slant it's refused ("Run into the hill square to its side for a tunnel mouth").
4. **Down into the felt** takes one new piece, the **tunnel mouth**: a straight 288 long that slopes down two levels in a wooden-lined cutting sunk into the board, with a portal at its foot. Below it, ordinary track at −96 runs under the felt. Another mouth, turned round, brings it back up.

The hill and the felt are never re-meshed for a tunnel: only the one-level blocks a cutting goes through (as ramps' cuttings are today, `HillCutsView`, only blocks whose cuts change), and a hole in the felt over each mouth's cutting (as underpasses', only when the mouths change).

## Q1. Through a hill: what happens on the other side

The line goes in through a cutting, under the hill, and out the far side through another cutting, wherever the ground comes back down to the line's level. An irregular hill is fine: a dip to one level in the middle is a cutting open to the sky (two more portals); a rise to three is just more roof. Refused, with a message:

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| A line square to the hill's side, a level-a-square hill two or more high | Cutting, portal, tunnel, portal, cutting, out |
| At a slant to the hill's side | "Run into the hill square to its side for a tunnel mouth" |
| The far side drops lower than the line (a terrace's edge) | "The far side is lower: the tunnel would come out in mid-air" |
| Longer than 20 squares (1440) | "Too long for a tunnel: the far side is more than 20 squares away" |
| A curve (or points) in the one-level part | Refused as today ("Too low — the hill is in the way"): cuttings are straight, as ramps' are |
| One level under the felt (−48) | "The hill is only one level over the line here: a tunnel needs two" |

**How it's placed** (options below): today a straight held into a hill says "The hill is a level up here: add a ramp before it". With Tunnels, it goes in instead (a cutting, then a tunnel), piece by piece, and a straight dropped against a hill's side can bore the whole way through in one go (`planTunnel`, prototyped: one walk ahead, the track rules for the few pieces).

## Q2. Into flat ground: cut and cover

A **tunnel mouth** (down), track at −96 under whatever is on top, a mouth turned round (up). A line on the felt crosses over it with nothing to build: no ramps, no bridge, no underpass. It's longer than a bridge crossing (two mouths, 576, plus what's under: at least a straight), but it's all on the ground.

- **Felt, roads, towns, rivers above:** the tunnel's own track goes under anything (a road, a street, a river, a field, a house), since nothing on the felt is in the way two levels down. Props (trees) on the felt don't block it.
- **The mouths themselves** are cuttings in the felt, so they're refused where a cutting can't go: on a road ("dip-road", the underpass's rule), in a river, on a building, field or station, and off the felt (on a hilltop, for now: "A tunnel mouth needs flat open ground, level with its top end").
- **Under a hill** the same track at −96 runs on (it's under two levels and more).

## Q3. Seeing what's underground

Trains in a tunnel are hidden by the hill or the felt, which is right, but players need to see where they are and where the line runs. Three treatments, the first two prototyped:

- **V1. Dashes over the tunnel** (prototyped): a dashed line painted on the ground above every tunnel, on the hilltop or the felt. Static: built from the layout, rebuilt only when the tunnels found change (keyed). One draw call.
- **V2. Ghost trains** (prototyped): a car in a tunnel is drawn again as a pale silhouette, but only where something is in front of it (depth test "greater"): through the hill or the felt, never over itself out in the open. Turned on and off per car as it goes in and out (one ground lookup per car per drawn frame, only while trains move: idle stays 0 frames). Extra draws only for cars in tunnels.
- **V3. Cut-away near the camera** (not prototyped): the hill turns see-through near the line when zoomed in. Clipping or re-meshing the hill per camera move breaks the "camera move alone doesn't redraw" rule; not recommended.

Also for phase 2: the track itself in a tunnel drawn as a ghost while a piece is held over it, or selected (V2's material on track), and the dashes tappable to select the track under them (to erase or move it).

## Q4. Rules

- **Curves** inside: yes (rule 1 is per point). **Cuttings**: plain straights only (straight, like ramps' cuttings).
- **Switches, crossings, signals** inside a tunnel: refuse in phase 2 ("Points need daylight: put them outside the tunnel"), since they can't be seen or tapped there. (The prototype doesn't refuse them yet.) A signal on the open line by a portal is fine.
- **Stations, depots, kits**: not in tunnels.
- **Max length**: 20 squares for `planTunnel`'s one-go bore. Laid piece by piece there's no limit; trains show as ghosts.
- **Land editing**: the Raise and Lower tools must refuse lowering a hill under two levels over a tunnel, or raising one over a cutting (phase 2; the same check as track resting on a square).
- **Cost**: research **Tunnels 12🪵 + 120🪙** ← bridges. Digging costs **coins**, not planks: 1🪙 per 18 mm of tunnel, half that for cutting, 30🪙 a mouth (a 432 tunnel about 30🪙, a cut-and-cover crossing about 90🪙). The tech-tree review found coins piling up with nothing to buy (1279 by 180 min); this gives them a use. The play-through reaches it by the player's path (a goal: "Bore a tunnel").
- **Saves**: one new piece type (`tunnel-mouth`). Everything else is ordinary track, at y = 0 inside a hill or y = −96 under the felt. Old saves load unchanged (no piece of theirs was ever underground: the rules refused it). A save with a tunnel in it needs this version.

## Options

### A. Tunnels are a rule; track goes into the ground by itself (recommended)

The rule above, with no tunnel piece for hills. A straight laid into a hill's side cuts and bores; laid on from there, piece by piece, the line goes on under the hill (curves too) and out. A straight dropped square against a hill's side bores straight through to the far side in one go (`planTunnel`, one undo step, a toast "Bored a tunnel through the hill"), like the underpass's auto-dig. **Draw** does the same: a drawn line meeting a hill at its own level goes through instead of stopping red ("Too steep to draw"). For flat ground, the **Tunnel mouth** on the Bridges shelf; later (phase 3) Draw can offer cut and cover where a drawn line meets another line or a road, as it swaps in a flat crossing today.

- For: nothing new to learn for hills (lay track where you want it to go); irregular hills just work; curves inside; one new piece; saves barely change.
- Against: the change is in every placement check (a little more work on a pointer move: measured below); "where's my track?" needs V1 and V2; a careless line into a hill now bores instead of being stopped (Game mode: it costs coins, and the ghost shows the price; the toast says what happened).

### B. A set of tunnel pieces

A **Portal** piece (a straight 72 long with the portal on it) for each end and **tunnel straights/curves** for inside, laid by hand. A portal is refused unless it's at a hill's side square; tunnel pieces only inside the hill; the far end needs another portal at the far side ("No hill side here for the portal"). Cut and cover: portal-down, tunnel pieces, portal-up.

- For: explicit; the tray says what a tunnel is.
- Against: four or more new piece types in saves and the tray; tedious through a stepped hill (portal, cutting pieces, tunnel pieces, cutting pieces, portal, by hand); the cutting still has to be cut; Draw has to learn all of them.

### C. One Tunnel piece that spans the hill

Like a Bridge spanning a river: drop **one Tunnel** on a hill's side and it stretches straight through to the far side (a piece whose length is set when it's put down), portals at both ends.

- For: the simplest for the player; one undo step.
- Against: no piece today has a length set on placement (geometry, saves, snapping and routing all assume fixed shapes); straight only; a stepped hill still needs the cuttings either side. A's `planTunnel` gives the player the same one-drop result with ordinary straights.

## Recommendation

**A**, with V1 (dashes) and V2 (ghost trains), cuttings for the one-level steps, the tunnel mouth for cut and cover, and coins as the price of digging. Phase 2 builds it out: research and costs, the refusals in Q4, the one-go bore on drop with its toast and undo, Draw through hills, the held/selected ghost, land-edit guards, an e2e on a phone on the player's path (a train carrying goods through a tunnel to a town, not just a portal drawn), and the play-through reaching "Bore a tunnel". Phase 3: Draw's cut and cover, mouths on hilltops (cut into the hill), curved cuttings.

## The prototype

Behind a switch (`setTunnels`, in `src/landscape/hill-shape.ts`), off by default: with it off nothing changes, and all tests pass as before.

- `TUNNEL_COVER` (96), and `tunnel-mouth` (288 long, down 96, flippable to come back up), `src/track`.
- Placement (`checkPlacement`'s `tunnelPlacement`): covered points pass whatever is over them; the "portal" (slant), "shallow" and "mouth" refusals. Support: track in a tunnel lies on its floor. Props don't block track under the felt.
- Cuttings: `cutsHills` takes plain straights, as a slot with upright sides that leaves blocks two levels over the line whole (`HillShape`).
- `planTunnel` (`src/game/tunnel-dig.ts`): the straight bore through a hill, and its refusals.
- `TunnelsView` (`src/render/tunnels-view.ts`): portals (a painted frame and a dark mouth, at each hill side and each mouth's foot) and the dashes; the mouth's sunk cutting (`track-geometry.ts`) and its hole in the felt (`underpassHoles`); the ghost cars (`TrainView.underground`).
- `src/game/tunnels.test.ts`, 12 tests: placement on and off, the slant, the cutting slot and what it leaves whole, cut and cover two levels down (one refused), a road over it, the mouth's ground, support, a line crossing over at ground level, `planTunnel` through a stepped hill and refused at a slant and over a drop, the portals and dashes found.
- `tools/shots/tunnels.mjs`: a hill raised with the Raise tool's land editor (a level a square, up to three), a line bored through it by `planTunnel`, a cut-and-cover crossing laid by the editor (each piece checked by the game's own rules first), trains run by the sim's own ticks: the hill train in at y 0 and out the far side; the goods train down the mouth to −96, under the line on top, every car at −96 there.

**Costs** (prototype, headless software GL, seed-24 Sandbox map with the two test lines: 25 pieces):

| What | Tunnels off | Tunnels on |
|---|---|---|
| Track rules for a straight held into the hill (a pointer move) | 0.011 ms | 0.105 ms |
| Cuttings set again on a track change (every plain straight now asked) | 0.005 ms | 0.195 ms |

## Risks

- **Pointer-move cost** of the extra ground lookups in the placement check, and the cuttings now asking every plain straight (cheap to gate by a bounding-box hill check if it shows).
- **Ghosts** add draws per car in a tunnel and overlap themselves (a pale silhouette, not a clean outline); a stencil outline would be cleaner and costs a pass.
- **A cutting's edge**: the slot touches the two-level block beside it only at the edge, so that block is drawn as a cut column (a hair of slope at the portal's top, about a grid step); fixable in `cutBlocks`'s sampling.
- **Selection and erasing** underground track needs the dashes to be tappable.
- **Land edits over tunnels** (Q4).

## Decision

The owner chose **A**, and one change: hills step a level a square and are mostly one level (on 20 maps, of hill squares: 76% one level, 23% two, 1.4% three), so tunnels needing two levels of hill over the felt's level would be rare and short. "(a) let tunnels cut a covered trench, or use the tunnel mouth to go down, so a 1-level hill works." Plus the rules in Q4, the ghost for held and selected tunnel track, a cheap gate and timing on a large layout, the ghost overlap fixed.

## Built (phase 2)

**The rule, as built.** Track is in a tunnel where the ground over its base is at least two levels (`TUNNEL_COVER`, 96): a hill and the board both count. So:

- **Under a hill a level high (or more), a level under the felt (−48).** The hill's level and the board's make two. This is the usual tunnel.
- **Under anything, two levels under the felt (−96)**: cut and cover, under roads, rivers, towns, fields and other track.
- **No cuttings.** Phase 1's slot through a one-level step is gone: the line goes *under* the step instead, so the hills are never re-meshed for a tunnel (nor is the felt, but a hole over each mouth's cutting). Phase 1's "portal-top slope" went with it.

**Pieces.** Two, on the Track tab's Bridges shelf, both flippable (their ends swap, to come back up):

| Piece | Length | Down | For | Game mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short tunnel mouth (`tunnel-mouth-short`) | 144 (a ramp's) | 48 | Under a hill | 1🪵 + 20🪙 at the workshop |
| Tunnel mouth (`tunnel-mouth`) | 288 | 96 | Under anything on the felt | 2🪵 + 30🪙 |

A mouth goes on flat felt at its top end. Its foot may run on under a hill's side, square to it, up to where the trains still fit under the hill (the planner puts it 1 to 18 mm in); a portal stands there on the hill's side. Elsewhere its portal stands at its foot, in its cutting. Everything else in a tunnel is ordinary track (`tunnelTrack`: plain straights, curves and S-curves).

**Research.** Tunnels: tier 2, after Bridges, **12🪵 + 120🪙**, built (no longer "soon"). The coins for the digging are the mouths' price at the workshop (a tunnel under a hill takes two short ones: 40🪙), so undo gives the mouths back to the box, as every piece. Sandbox: always on.

**Placing** (all one undo step each):

- **A straight dropped against a hill's side** (a new plain straight from the tray, run into a one-level hill at the felt's level): a tunnel goes down in its place (`planBore` → `planTunnel`): plain straights from the line's end to a short mouth whose foot is just under the hill's side, straights a level under all the way under the hill, a short mouth up out of the far side, and a straight on. Toast: "Dug a tunnel under the hill". If the hill is too near the line's end for a mouth, the line's last plain straight or two (in line) are taken up and the tunnel laid from further back. In Game mode it needs two short mouths in the toy box ("A tunnel takes two short tunnel mouths: make them at the workshop").
- **Draw across a hill a level high**: under it. With ramps in the box, the line climbs as before as the finger goes onto the hill; when it comes back down the far side (ramp down), the climb is swapped for the tunnel (`DrawLine.underHill`). With no ramps to climb with (a new Game), it goes under as soon as it meets the hill. A line drawn onto the hill and stopped there still climbs. Two-level and higher hills: under them too, a level under the felt all the way (every point under the hill has at least a level over it).
- **By hand**: a mouth, then straights or curves from its foot. One level under the felt with no hill over it is refused: "Not deep enough for a tunnel: it needs two levels of ground over it. Under a hill go one level down, under the felt two".

**Refused, as tested:**

| Case | Message |
|---|---|
| Points, crossings, signals pieces, stations in a tunnel | "Only plain track goes in a tunnel: points, signals and stations need daylight" |
| A signal dragged (or the Signal tool) onto a joint in a tunnel | "Signals need daylight: put it outside the tunnel" |
| A line meeting the hill's side at a slant | "Meet the hill's side square for a tunnel" |
| The hill dips to the felt on the way through | "The hill dips to the felt on the way through: tunnel under one hill at a time" |
| More than 20 squares under the hill | "Too long for a tunnel: the far side is more than 20 squares away" |
| A mouth on a hill, across a road, in a river | "A tunnel mouth goes on flat felt, its low end up to a hill's side or under the felt" / the underpass's road message / river |
| Raise or Lower a square over a tunnel | "There's track on or beside it" (the land editor's rule already counts track under the ground) |

**Seeing it.** Portals (painted, the bridges' red, with a dark mouth) where track goes under the ground; dashes on the ground over every tunnel; cars in a tunnel drawn as a flat cream silhouette through the hill or felt (opaque, so no overlap: the phase 1 ghost blended its parts over each other); while a track piece is held, the track in tunnels drawn through the ground too, and a held piece under the ground seen through it; a selected piece in a tunnel has its outline through the ground. All static or per car per drawn frame: 0 frames idle.

**Costs** (e2e, headless software GL):

| What | ms |
|---|---|
| Track rules for a straight held near a tunnel (a pointer move) | 0.001–0.002 |
| The same on open felt | 0.003 |
| Tunnels found again on a track change, the game's layout (25 pieces) | 0.2–1.1 |
| Tunnels found on a 1,100-piece stress layout over the map | 1.4–4.8 |
| Frames drawn idle (2 s) with tunnels, portals and a train standing | 0 |

The gate: a piece is sampled only if it's under the felt, a mouth, or near a hill two levels above its base (`mayTunnel`: the grid's heights in its reach). Phase 1's 0.105 ms per pointer move and 0.195 ms of cuttings per change are gone (no cuttings).

**Saves.** Two new piece types. Track under the ground is ordinary track at y −48 or −96. Old saves load unchanged.
